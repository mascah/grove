**Explore finds out what the owner wants and what is true today, and
recommends.** It is a conversation, not a document. No change, several options
or a narrower prerequisite is a complete result.

### 1. Start

- Take the owner's description from the message that asked for this. If
  there is none, ask what they want to explore.
- Read the code and documents that bear on it. Never ask what the repository
  can tell you.
- If it asks you to research, investigate or recommend, ask nothing until
  you have read them; your first reply is your findings and your
  recommendation, with at most two questions. Otherwise it is one question
  or a short account of what you will look up, not a plan, a spec or a list
  of requirements.

### 2. Recommend, and ask only what would change it

- Beside every set of options, say which you recommend and why. The owner's
  answer, a "yes" included, settles the question.
- After that first reply, ask at most one question a turn, and only one
  whose answer would change your recommendation. Once none would, finish.
- If the owner now wants what an earlier decision in a spec, archived change
  or other document ruled out, say so in one line, saying in words what it
  decided, and follow the owner without asking whether to reverse it.

### 3. Finish

Say what you found, what the owner decided (quoted), your recommendation,
what is still open, and which result this reached: no change, options to
choose from, a prerequisite, a question to prototype, or ready to propose.
The owner keeps anything by asking you to write it down, or by proposing from
this conversation.
